abstract: "This paper presents Max Weber’s understanding of capitalism and the law by tracing their sociological, religious, and institutional foundations in “Die Protestantische Ethik und der „Geist“ des Kapitalismus“ and “Wirtschaft und Gesellschaft”. It demonstrates how the Protestant ethic transformed traditional values and rationalized utilitarianism that precedes and sustains capitalism. Weber conceptualizes law as an evolving system of norms backed by the state’s coercive apparatus, continuously legitimizing rule through formal predictability rather than religious tradition. Finally, the paper assesses the relevance of Weber’s framework in modern societies, arguing that while changing norms have given way to modern consumer culture, the underlying dynamics of institutional rationalization remain central to understanding modern economies and representative democracies."
